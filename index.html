About

I'm an undergraduate student at the University of Toronto studying Astronomy, Physics, and Mathematics. My research interests broadly lie in observational and transient astrophysics, particularly radio astronomy and fast radio bursts (FRBs).

Much of my recent work has focused on understanding FRBs and the environments they come from. My current work, through the Summer Undergraduate Research Program (SURP) at the Dunlap Institute for Astronomy and Astrophysics, focuses on searches for persistent radio sources associated with FRBs. I have also worked on improving the localization of FRBs detected in the far sidelobes of CHIME, along with other projects within the CHIME/FRB Collaboration. I am especially interested in using multi-wavelength observations to better understand the sources and environments behind these still-mysterious bursts.

Beyond research, I'm passionate about astronomy outreach and making science accessible to a wider community. I currently serve as President of the University of Toronto's Astronomy & Space Exploration Association (ASX), a 1,000+ member student organization, where I help organize astronomy events, observing nights, and outreach initiatives.

Outside of astronomy, I enjoy biking, thrifting, exploring coffee shops, reading, and learning about history, philosophy, and archaeology.

Tracing Star Formation Histories in Vela

AST430 Research Project (A+) · Dr. Ronan Kerr, Prof. Joshua Speagle

Investigated the formation and evolution of young stellar populations in the Vela Complex using Gaia astrometry, stellar age fitting, and orbital traceback to identify possible shared formation histories.

Ongoing

CHIME/FRB Far Sidelobe Localization

AST431 Research Project (A+) · Prof. Juan Mena-Parra

Developed and tested localization methods for FRBs detected in CHIME’s far sidelobes by characterizing instrumental systematics and evaluating geometric corrections. Applied these improved localizations to support probabilistic host-galaxy association and follow-up.

Summer 2025

CHIME Outriggers FRB Localization

Dunlap Institute for Astronomy & Astrophysics, University of Toronto · Prof. Juan Mena-Parra

Developed and tested clock-synchronization techniques for the CHIME Outriggers, using noise modelling, interpolation, and timing-error analysis to improve FRB localization. Explored a new localization approach for mitigating timing uncertainties between telescope stations.
